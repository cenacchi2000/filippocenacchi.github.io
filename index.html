Filippo Cenacchi

Doctoral Researcher in Computer Science at Frontier AI Research Centre at Macquarie University.

My research focuses on embodied AI for diagnostic medicine, combining humanoid robots, multimodal machine learning, clinical avatars, and interactive mental-health assessment. I build simulation-first diagnostic systems where humanoid agents, virtual patients, and multimodal AI support earlier detection of depression, PTSD, dementia, cognitive decline, and broader affective-health conditions.

My work reframes diagnosis as an adaptive process of evidence collection, counterfactual simulation, targeted questioning, and interpretable decision support. I am supervised by Distinguished Chair Professor Longbing Cao and Professor Deborah Richards.

Research interests

  • Artificial intelligence, machine learning, and data-driven decision support
  • Human-computer interaction, human-centred AI, and responsible technology design
  • Multimodal and embodied AI using speech, vision, language, and interaction signals
  • Applied AI for healthcare, education, software engineering, and real-world decision-making

More broadly, I am interested in building intelligent, human-centred systems that connect advanced AI methods with practical impact across clinical, educational, organisational, and societal settings.

Research Focus

Humanoid AI

Humanoid-AI Diagnostic Agents

Designing conversational humanoid agents (e.g., Ameca) that sense and fuse facial, vocal, and linguistic cues to support early detection of depression, PTSD, and cognitive decline. Emphasis on trust calibration, safety, and clinician-style pacing.

Explore project↗
Simulation

Simulation-First Training

SimClinician: a simulation testbed with MetaHuman patients, synchronized speech/face/gaze and PHQ-8 / PCL-C flows. Enables counterfactual replay, nonverbal perturbations, and uncertainty-aware turn management.

Explore project↗
3D Clinical Avatar

LLM-Guided Clinical Avatar

A multi-scale digital twin prototype that links a 3D anatomical avatar, an interview-driven risk model and CT/MRI-derived organ meshes into a single interactive simulation.

Explore project↗
Triage AI

Decision Support & Triage

Interactive triage wizards and RL-inspired probing layered over E-DAIC and DAIC-WOZ to select targeted questions that maximize diagnostic clarity while exposing confidence and evidence.

Explore project↗
Dementia

Passive Dementia Screening

Language-free facial micro-dynamics pipeline for early dementia screening from in-the-wild talking-head video, using blink, gaze, and jaw dynamics with a calibrated, deployable risk score.

Explore project↗
Demo

MetaHuman Avatar Synchronised with Ameca

Demo of a MetaHuman avatar of the Ceo of Nvidia driven by Ameca real-time body signals. Showcases synchronised facial motion, gaze and pacing for simulation-first training.

Chieri Ishikawa
Summer Research Intern · Parkinson's disease modelling
chieri.ishikawa@students.mq.edu.au

Filippo was a very supportive supervisor during my summer research internship on predicting Parkinson's disease progression. He gave us a challenging and meaningful research problem using the PPMI dataset, while giving us enough direction to explore the problem and make our own decisions. He pointed us towards relevant prior work and modelling approaches, and often suggested experiments that we would not have thought to run ourselves, such as ablation experiments to understand how much the model depended on each modality. What I particularly valued was that his guidance encouraged us to think beyond simply improving model performance and to question what our results actually meant. I also really appreciated his feedback on how we presented our work, especially his emphasis on making visualisations understandable at a glance even to non-technical readers. Through his supervision, I became more confident not only technically, but also in how I approach, interpret, and communicate research. I'm very grateful for his support throughout the internship and would gladly work with him again.

Tuong Vy Vu
Summer Research Intern · Parkinson's disease modelling
tuongvy.vu@students.mq.edu.au

I had the opportunity to work with Filippo during Macquarie University's 2025–2026 FSE Summer Research Internship, where he supervised my project on modelling Parkinson's disease severity using the Parkinson's Progression Markers Initiative (PPMI) dataset. As this was my first time working with a large medical dataset, Filippo's guidance was particularly valuable. He encouraged us to begin with exploratory data analysis (EDA), which helped us understand the data and identify the key attributes and modalities needed for our modelling task before moving on to model development. He also introduced us to Transformer-based models, which were not really new but that's the first time that me and my co-intern implement it, and guided us through implementing them for disease severity prediction. I especially found his advice to evaluate our new models using different metrics and datasets useful, as it encouraged us to think beyond simply getting a model to work and instead consider how well it generalised. Overall, Filippo provided clear research direction while giving us the opportunity to explore and learn independently, and his guidance helped me develop both my technical skills and my understanding of the research process.
